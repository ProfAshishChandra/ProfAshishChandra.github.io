## Robust and Stochastic Optimization

This research develops mathematical models, formulations, and algorithms for decision-making in systems affected by uncertainty. A central focus is on robust and adjustable optimization, stochastic optimization, risk measures, chance-constrained optimization, and optimization problems with discrete or nonlinear structure.

The methodological objective is to represent uncertainty in a way that preserves meaningful operational detail while retaining computational tractability. The work therefore studies decomposition, relaxation, convexification, dynamic programming, and optimization-based bounding techniques. These methods are useful for problems in which decisions must remain effective across many possible future states and where nominal or average-case solutions can be misleading.

The research also addresses performance certification and risk-aware decision making. Rather than only computing an optimized decision under one assumed distribution or scenario, the goal is often to determine what can be guaranteed, what worst-case performance is possible, how tail outcomes should be measured, and what additional structural assumptions are required to obtain useful bounds.

## Supply Chain Network Resilience

This research examines how supply chain and service networks can be designed, operated, and validated when disruptions and uncertainty affect facilities, transportation links, demand, and operating conditions. Topics include network reliability, partial and multi-state failures, worst-case system performance, resilient network design, service loss, sustainability and carbon tradeoffs, and adaptive operational decisions following disruption.

A recurring theme is the use of optimization-based network models to quantify the operational consequences of disruption rather than relying only on nominal performance measures. This includes studying which disruption scenarios are performance-critical, how much demand can still be served when parts of the network fail, how capacity or network structure should be changed to improve resilience, and how strategic decisions interact with recourse decisions after uncertainty is realized.

Current application directions include healthcare and pharmaceutical supply chains, geopolitical and trade uncertainty, and service–carbon tradeoffs. These settings require models that can simultaneously represent network structure, uncertainty, operational adaptation, and system-level performance.

## Risk, Reliability, and Performance Analytics

This research develops quantitative approaches for measuring, validating, and controlling adverse system outcomes. It includes optimization-based methods for identifying performance-critical disruption scenarios, counting and characterizing failure configurations, evaluating risk measures, and certifying system reliability.

A major methodological theme is the interaction between combinatorial uncertainty and an embedded operational optimization problem. In network systems, a disruption configuration does not directly determine performance; performance is often obtained only after solving a flow, allocation, routing, or other optimization model. This creates challenging problems in exact scenario counting, probabilistic certification, and worst-case validation.

The work combines mathematical programming, applied probability, statistics, dynamic programming, and prescriptive analytics. Applications include supply chains, communication and transportation networks, financial networks, and other systems in which rare but consequential events can determine overall reliability.
