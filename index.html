Research

Optimization for Resilient, Sustainable, and Reliable Systems

We develop mathematical models, algorithms, and data-driven decision tools to design and manage complex networked systems under uncertainty, with applications in supply chains and beyond.

Supply Chain Resilience and Sustainable Operations

We study the design and operation of supply-chain networks that remain effective under disruptions while advancing environmental sustainability. This includes network design, capacity planning, disruption mitigation, and service–carbon tradeoffs.

Supply Chain Resilience and Sustainable Operations

A central question in this research is how a supply-chain network should be designed, evaluated, and operated when disruptions remove capacity or routing options. The work treats the supply chain as a networked decision system and studies questions such as which disruption scenarios are most damaging, how network structure affects performance, where additional capacity or redundancy is most valuable, and how service objectives interact with environmental goals.

This perspective supports both validation—understanding how well a network can perform under adverse conditions—and prescription—identifying design or operating decisions that improve resilience, service, and sustainability.

Risk, Reliability, and Decision-Making Under Uncertainty

Complex systems can face very large spaces of uncertain events, while only a relatively small subset may cause unacceptable performance. This research develops optimization and probability tools for quantifying those events, estimating their likelihood, identifying critical scenarios, and incorporating risk directly into decision models.

The broader objective is to move beyond average-case performance and provide mathematically grounded ways to certify, stress-test, and improve decisions in settings where failures, demand, and other uncertain factors can materially change system outcomes.

Optimization Methods for Complex and Large-Scale Problems

Many decision problems of practical interest are difficult because they are nonconvex, combinatorial, constrained, or embedded within larger network models. This research develops algorithmic and modeling tools that exploit mathematical structure to obtain tractable formulations, useful bounds, decomposition schemes, and scalable solution methods.

Methodological themes include primal–dual algorithms, relaxation and convexification, decomposition, dynamic programming, and optimization-based sampling or counting. These methods are motivated by difficult applications, but are developed with an emphasis on generality, computational efficiency, and theoretical guarantees.
